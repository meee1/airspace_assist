    Pre-flight brief (Plan check)

    Plan check, under the flight plan's leg list, opens the Pre-flight brief: everything along your plan at a planned altitude, speed and departure time, in one list you can copy or print.

    Inputs

    All are kept on the phone. Tap Check to run it again after a change.

    BoxUse
    Altitude ftPlanned altitude for legs without their own altitude (leg overrides from Edit are used where set).
    GS kt (TAS kt with a wind)Your speed. With a wind chosen it's taken as TAS and each leg gets its own groundspeed.
    Windnone; entered: one wind for the route (from °T, kt) read off the grid point wind chart; nearest TAF: each leg takes the surface wind forecast by the closest aerodrome TAF in your briefing at the time you'd be there.
    Departure (local)Your departure time. ETAs, tower hours, NOTAM times, SIGMETs and day/night use it.
    Burn L/h, Fuel LFuel flow and fuel on board, for the time and fuel table.
    Reserve minYour own final reserve. Empty = the CASA figure.
    OATOutside air temperature for hover performance where no TAF or METAR gives one.

    A TAF wind is the wind on the ground, not at your altitude; the NAIPS text briefing has no winds aloft. An entered wind from the grid point wind chart is the better figure.

    The brief at the top

    • NAIPS briefing: when it was issued, warned when over 6 hours old, ending before you arrive, or missing one of the plan's aerodromes.
    • Weather at each plan aerodrome at its ETA: TAF category, wind and weather then, with any TEMPO/INTER/PROB; the METAR; the ATIS.
    • Area QNH, and the number of SIGMETs/AIRMETs on the route at your level.

    Along the route

    In order, with distance and ETA:

    • waypoints (a coast leg's turn points are left out);
    • the CTAF or tower of the nearest aerodrome as it changes: tower hours at your ETA, the split-tower side, the call name ("Barwon Heads traffic"); broadcast areas;
    • area or ATC frequency changes at your altitude (class, limits, "clearance required"); zones list their ATIS frequency; the squawk at a tower where you land;
    • restricted, prohibited and danger areas you'd enter at that altitude, with activity and status at ETA;
    • NOTAM temporary zones, and NOTAM obstacles within 1 NM of track;
    • SIGMETs/AIRMETs at your planned level at ETA;
    • last light, if the plan ends after it.

    Wind by leg

    With a wind chosen: each leg's heading (°M), groundspeed and time. A coast leg is one row: "track along the coast", "HDG varies", average groundspeed.

    Time and fuel

    With Burn L/h (and optionally Fuel L): time and fuel per leg, fuel remaining at the end of each leg, and totals. Rows below the reserve are red. Leg times use the wind when one is chosen.

    Final reserve

    The CASA final reserve (Part 91 MOS 19.02), VFR:

    DayNight
    Helicopter20 min30 min
    Aeroplane30 min45 min
    • The category is the Aeroplane / Helicopter choice in Radio calls.
    • It's night when the departure or the arrival is outside first to last light, using the Departure time.
    • The fuel line says which applies, e.g. "reserve 20 min (helicopter, VFR day, CASA Part 91 MOS 19.02) = 33 L".
    • Enter Reserve min to use your own figure. The line then says "your setting" and notes when it's below the CASA minimum. Clear the box to go back.

    This is the fixed final reserve only. Add any variable or contingency fuel your operator requires.

    LSALT

    For each leg: the highest terrain or NOTAM obstacle within 2 NM of track, +1,000 ft, rounded up. If terrain isn't loaded for part of a leg it says "terrain partly loaded" and retries while the window is open. This is a guide from 30″ terrain data, not a published LSALT.

    NOTAMs by leg

    Aerodrome NOTAMs are listed under the leg arriving there (departure under leg 1); area NOTAMs under the first leg that passes within 1 NM; the rest under "Other".

    Weight and balance

    With a W&B profile set up, a row gives the take-off, landing and zero-fuel result against the envelope (see Weight and balance).

    Hover performance

    When your W&B profile has hover ceiling tables (see Hover performance inputs), the brief has a Hover performance table for the departure and every point you land at:

    ColumnFrom
    ETAThe plan's time there.
    TemperatureThe TAF at ETA, else the METAR, else the OAT you enter in Plan check.
    QNHTAF, METAR or area QNH; else 1013 hPa assumed, and the table says so.
    Pressure altitudeAerodrome elevation + (1013 − QNH) × 30 ft.
    Density altitudePressure altitude + 120 ft per °C above ISA.
    WeightYour W&B take-off weight less the fuel burnt to that point.
    IGE / OGE maxThe maximum weights from your hover ceiling tables at that density altitude.
    MarginHow far under the limit you are.
    • Amber when the margin is under 5%.
    • Red when you're over the limit, or the density altitude is outside your table.

    Density altitude here is an approximation. The flight manual's hover charts govern. The app has no performance figures of its own: the table only uses the numbers you copied from your flight manual.

    Power lines and obstacles

    Every power line the route crosses (Geoscience Australia transmission lines, 66 kV and up) and every NOTAM obstacle within 0.5 NM of it, in order along the route:

    • Power lines: how high you'll be above the ground where you cross it, at the leg's planned altitude. The lines' heights aren't in the data, so it can't give a clearance over the wires themselves. Amber under 500 ft above the ground.
    • Obstacles: height AMSL, how far off track, and your clearance at the planned altitude. Amber under 500 ft, red when the obstacle is above you.

    The same marks are on the plan's elevation profile and the map's side view.

    AIP Supplements and AICs

    The current SUPs and AICs for the plan's aerodromes, and ones placed within 10 NM of the route (wind farms, restricted areas, reporting points), with their dates. Below them, folded, the general ones with no place (e.g. amendments to the DAH or ERSA GEN). Titles only: read each on the Airservices website. See Aerodrome card.

    Ready to fly without signal?

    At the end, the brief checks this device has:

    • the app and AIP data;
    • terrain along the route (3 NM either side);
    • chart tiles for each ticked sheet the route crosses;
    • a NAIPS briefing under 6 hours old and valid to arrival.

    It names chart sheets on the route you haven't ticked, with a Tick them button. Coast legs: "turn points stored with the plan".

    Copy and print

    Copy text copies the whole brief as plain text. Print / PDF opens it for printing or saving as a PDF.
