    Radio audio and transcription

    The app can listen to the radio through your headset or the audio panel and write each transmission down, flag calls with your callsign, offer the ATIS and clearance details for your notes, capture an ATIS and check your read-back. Everything is in Options → Radio audio and transcription.

    Transcription and suggestions are an aid only. The speech model mishears callsigns and numbers. Your ears and your read-back come first. Nothing is filled in without your tap: check it against what you heard.

    1. Audio input test

    First check what audio the tablet can actually hear.

    1. Connect the headset (a Bluetooth headset such as the Bose A30), or a cable from the audio panel through a USB-C audio adapter.
    2. Tap List inputs (allow the microphone once) and pick the input. Bluetooth and USB inputs are named as such.
    3. Tap Listen and watch the meter while you talk on the intercom, then while the radio plays an ATIS or a CTAF call.

    A Bluetooth headset may send the tablet only its own boom microphone (in "call" mode), not the radio and intercom audio: the meter and a 30 s recording show what actually arrives. If the radio isn't there, use a cable from the audio panel's recording or music output into a USB-C audio adapter.

    • Meter moves for the radio: radio audio reaches the tablet.
    • Moves only when you talk: the headset sends just your microphone. Use the audio panel cable instead.
    • Raw audio (ticked by default) turns off the phone's echo cancelling, noise suppression and automatic gain, which can cut radio audio as "noise".
    • Record 30 s, Play back and Save recording let you hear what the tablet got.

    A Bluetooth headset sends call-quality audio while the tablet listens to it. Nothing leaves the device (except with the online speech service, below).

    The browser engine listens to the input picked in the audio test (Chrome; an older browser uses the default microphone and says so). Both engines are told local names so they come out right: aerodromes within 30 NM (with Tower/Ground/Approach where ERSA has them), VFR points within 20 NM, the current area unit, and your type and callsign. Without that, "Moorabbin Tower … Carrum" came out "Murabintaur … Karam". The log, alerts, Use suggestions, ATIS capture, read-back check, flight-log transcripts and hands-free checklists work with either engine.

    On the device: the app asks the browser about en-AU, en-US and en-GB and uses the first one it has an offline pack for (or offers Install offline pack for one it can download). The grey line lists each answer. Try on the device anyway asks for on-device recognition even when the browser says no; if it refuses, it falls back to the online service and says so.

    If browser recognition doesn't work: the grey line under Engine shows what the browser offers (recognition, offline pack, whether it can take the chosen input) and the last recognition events as they happen: start, audiostart, speechstart, hearing / result, error: …, end. If it can't take the chosen input, the app closes its own input test while recognition listens, because a phone can't usually give the microphone to two things at once; recognition then hears the phone's default microphone (a Bluetooth headset's, when one is connected for calls).

    3. Transcribe

    • Tick Transcribe the input. Each transmission (picked out from the background hiss) becomes a line with its time, newest first.
    • Sensitivity (Low / Normal / High) sets how much louder than the hiss a sound must be to count. Higher catches quieter calls but also more noise.
    • Your callsign is highlighted; frequencies, squawk codes, QNH, heights, runways and the ATIS letter are bold. Spoken numbers ("three four zero one", "one thousand five hundred") are read as digits.
    • The last two lines (within 2 minutes) show on a RADIO row in the top bar, which stays while Options is closed. Tap it for the whole log. Turn the row off under Top bar tiles.
    • It keeps listening when Options is closed, until you untick it.
    • Transcribe the recording does the same for the 30 s recording. Copy puts the log on the clipboard; Clear empties it.

    Your callsign heard

    When a transmission has your callsign (the last three letters, spoken phonetically or as letters, from Radio calls), the tablet vibrates twice, plays a two-note chime (through the headset when that's the sound output) and marks the RADIO row orange for 30 s. Turn it off with Alert when my callsign is heard.

    Use suggestions

    A Use button appears on the log line and the RADIO row when a transmission has:

    • an ATIS letter ("information Charlie") and/or QNH;
    • and, with your callsign: a squawk code, a level ("not above 1,500", "climb to 3,000", "maintain…", "flight level 95") and a "cleared …" route.

    Tap Use to put them in the ATIS and clearance notes. The ATIS goes to the aerodrome named in the transmission, else the one nearby. Nothing is filled in without the tap.

    ATIS capture

    In the ATIS and clearance window there's a Listen to ATIS button. It needs the input set up as above.

    1. Tune the ATIS on the radio.
    2. Tap Listen to ATIS. The app listens through the chosen engine for up to 2 minutes, or until the broadcast repeats. Stop ends early.
    3. It shows what it heard as fields: Information (letter), Runway, Wind, Visibility, Cloud, Temperature, QNH, Other.
    4. Tap Use to save the letter, QNH and the fields as the ATIS for that aerodrome.

    The saved ATIS shows in the dialog and in the aerodrome card's Weather section as ATIS heard hhmmZ, and the letter goes into your calls. The ATIS text doesn't go into the radio log.

    An aid only: check each field against what you hear.

    Read-back check

    With transcription on and Check my read-back ticked (on by default):

    • When a clearance or instruction to your callsign is heard with a squawk, level, heading, QNH, runway or frequency, the next transmission within 30 s that has your callsign or those items is treated as your read-back and compared.
    • The log line and RADIO row show Read-back ✓, or what differs or is missing, e.g.:
      Read-back: not above 1500 (cleared 1000) · missed squawk 3401
    • A mismatch gives a short vibration.

    It can only compare what both transmissions were heard as, and it can't tell who is speaking. A ✓ doesn't mean your read-back was right; a mismatch may be the model mishearing. Your own ears decide.

    In the flight log

    Each transcribed line is kept with its time and position (not in the simulator). Each flight's GPX (Flight log button, Options → Flight logs, Google Drive) has them as waypoints named "Radio 0412Z" with the words as the description. Replay shows them on the RADIO row at their time.

    Memory

    The transcription section shows how much memory the speech model takes: about 290 MB for the standard model once it has run, on top of the app. It's freed when the app is closed or the model deleted. Options → Render timing has a memory line too (the app's own memory, the speech model, roughly the device's RAM, and how much is stored).
