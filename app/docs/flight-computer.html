    Flight computer (training)

    More → Flight computer is an E6B-style mechanical flight computer to practise with, in three tabs: Computer, Wind and Problems. It always shows in a dark theme.

    It's a training aid. Use your own computer and the app's figures for real planning.

    Computer side

    The circular slide rule.

    • Drag the blue disc to turn it. Drag on the outer scale to move the red cursor.
    • Outer scale: miles or litres. Inner scale: minutes. The ▲ 60 is the speed index. An hours ring sits inside the minutes.
    • Speed sets the index to your groundspeed (or Plan check's speed). Fuel sets it to Plan check's burn. Reset.
    • Readout gives the values over the index and at the cursor. Untick it to practise reading the scales yourself.

    Time, speed, distance and fuel

    Put the speed (or burn per hour) on the outer scale over the ▲ 60. Then every distance (or fuel) on the outer scale sits over its time on the inner scale. Example: 60 under 110 kt, and 55 NM sits over 30 min.

    Density altitude and TAS windows

    • In the window at the top of the disc, set the OAT (°C, on the disc) against the pressure altitude (thousands of feet, showing through from the base).
    • CAS on the inner scale then reads TAS on the outer scale.
    • The DENS ALT pointer in the other window reads density altitude.
    • Both follow the standard atmosphere (ISA), so they agree with the 120 ft per °C rule of thumb.

    Conversion arrows

    • Red arrows on the rim. Put a value (inner scale) under one arrow and read it (inner scale) under another of the same kind: NAUT / STAT / KM, LITRES / US GAL, KG / LB.
    • Fuel weight: put the disc's FUEL L arrow under AVGAS KG (0.72 kg/L) or JET KG (0.80 kg/L), then read kg (outer) over litres (inner). Use your fuel docket's density when you have it.

    Wind side

    Worked the "wind dot up" way.

    • Drag the dark ring to turn the plate. Drag inside it to slide the grid.
    • ✎ Mark puts the wind dot: press on the centre line and slide. A label shows the knots, and the dot snaps to the centre line and to whole knots. In a problem, once the plate is on the wind a plain tap marks the dot. Erase removes it.
    • The grid's arcs count from the grid's centre: with 100 under the grommet, 15 kt up is the 115 arc.
    • The readout gives the course under the TRUE INDEX, the groundspeed under the grommet, and the dot's arc and drift.
    • Crosswind shows a square grid round the grommet for crosswind and headwind components.

    Worked example: wind

    True course 157°, TAS 90 kt, wind 270°T / 15 kt.

    1. Turn the plate until 270 is under the TRUE INDEX.
    2. Slide the grid so a convenient arc (say 100) is under the grommet, and mark the wind dot 15 kt straight up (on the 115 arc).
    3. Turn the plate until the course, 157, is under the TRUE INDEX.
    4. Slide the grid until the dot sits on the TAS arc, 90.
    5. Read the groundspeed under the grommet: about 95 kt.
    6. Read the drift at the dot: about 9° right of the centre line. The wind is from the right, so steer into it: heading = 157 + 9 = 166°T.

    (Wind triangle: WCA 8.8° R, GS 94.8 kt.) Apply variation for a magnetic heading.

    Worked example: TAS and density altitude

    Pressure altitude 4,000 ft, OAT +20 °C, CAS 100 kt.

    1. In the window, set +20 against pressure altitude 4 (thousand).
    2. Read DENS ALT: about 5,500 ft (the standard atmosphere gives 5,490). Check: ISA at 4,000 ft is +7 °C, so it's 13° above ISA; 4,000 + 13 × 120 ≈ 5,560 ft, close enough for the rule of thumb.
    3. Find 100 (CAS) on the inner scale and read TAS above it on the outer scale: about 109 kt.

    Worked example: crosswind

    Runway 17 (170°M), ATIS wind 220°M / 20 kt.

    1. Turn the plate until 220 is under the index, and mark the dot 20 kt up from the grommet.
    2. Tap Crosswind for the square grid, and turn the runway heading, 170, under the index.
    3. Read the dot: across = crosswind, up = headwind (down = tailwind). Here the wind is 50° off the runway: about 15 kt crosswind from the right and 13 kt headwind.

    (20 × sin 50° = 15.3; 20 × cos 50° = 12.9.)
