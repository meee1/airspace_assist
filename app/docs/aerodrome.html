    Aerodrome card

    The Aerodrome card (the Aerodrome tab on the full-screen map) shows one aerodrome in detail: frequencies, tower status, runways and circuits, the ERSA FAC sheet, weather and NOTAMs.

    Which aerodrome is shown

    • Closest aerodrome by default. With Favour ahead on (More), aerodromes ahead of you are preferred over ones behind ("Closest ahead").
    • With a flight plan the card follows the plan:
      • the next plan aerodrome once you're within 10 NM, so the inbound or overflying call is ready at the CTAF call point;
      • from 15 NM when the active waypoint is one of that aerodrome's ERSA reporting points (e.g. ACE then YMMB), so the tower inbound call is ready;
      • the aerodrome you just left or overflew, out to 5 NM;
      • otherwise the closest.
    • Pinned: tap an aerodrome on the map or in Nearby, or pick one in search. The header shows it's selected and the CTAF tile gets a CARD … ✕ badge. Back to auto (or the ✕) returns to the closest.
    • The header says which rule applied.
    • Heliports and hospital landing sites are hidden unless Show heliports is on. Include non-ERSA strips adds strips from other sources (tagged).

    The CTAF tile at the top always follows where you are, even when the card is pinned or following the plan.

    The header

    Code, name, distance and an arrow pointing to it, plus the elevation. It sticks to the top while you scroll the card; once you've scrolled down it shrinks to one line. Tap the slim header to go back to the top of the card.

    CTAF and tower

    • CTAF from ERSA. If ERSA has none: a CTAF printed on the VTC/VNC (tagged VTC or VNC), else the broadcast area frequency, else the AIP default 126.7.
    • Towered aerodromes: ATIS, TWR, GND, DEL, APP, DEP from ERSA. Outside tower hours tower-only frequencies are struck through.
    • Key values as chips: CTR entry altitude, circuit altitude, helicopter altitude, reporting points.
    • Reporting points at tower aerodromes as chips with name, bearing and distance, nearest first. Tap one to go to it.
    • ERSA local procedures: the full ERSA text (communications notes, local traffic regulations, flight procedures). With Helicopter selected in Radio calls, helicopter items are highlighted.
    • Every value carries a source tag: green for AIP, orange for anything else. Where another source disagrees it's shown as a note, e.g. "OurAirports says …".

    Approach when passing

    For a towered aerodrome that isn't in your plan or Go to and that you're not inside the zone of, the CTAF tile shows its approach unit (by sector at your altitude where ERSA lists several). An aerodrome with no approach unit in ERSA (Moorabbin) shows the tower. Once it's in your plan or Go to, or you're within 3 NM in its zone, it goes back to TWR.

    Beyond 10 NM and broadcast areas

    With no aerodrome within 10 NM, the CTAF tile shows the broadcast area frequency (e.g. Abrolhos 127.1) or the area frequency at your altitude. The card still shows the closest aerodrome.

    Split towers

    Where ERSA's procedure text ties a tower frequency to a side, it is labelled with that side and the ERSA text is quoted under it. At Moorabbin, 118.1 is East ("DEP to the E call 118.1", "ARR from E must call 118.1") and 123.0 is West ("DEP to the W call 123.0", "report at CARR on 123.0 … western circuit").

    • Which side: your bearing from the field. Over the field: the side of your next plan waypoint; else the side you arrived from (kept 3 minutes); else your track after take-off; else "see ATIS".
    • Reporting-point rules win: "When RWY 17 or 35 in use, ARR VFR ACFT from the S should track via and report at CARR on 123.0" applies when you're heading for CARR, within 3 NM of it, or south of the field. So Carrum gives TWR W 123.0 · via CARR (RWY 17/35). Pick another runway in Radio calls and it goes back to the side rule.
    • Departing: the side of the first plan point after the aerodrome.

    ERSA's COMM line and the VTC only list "TWR 118.1 / 123.0". The East/West labels come from ERSA's procedure text. The ATIS has the final word.

    Tower hours

    • Read from ERSA's TWR HR notes, in local time with daylight saving by state. Unknown hours: the tower is assumed active.
    • Outside tower hours the card switches to CTAF calls. Where ERSA says plainly "Outside TWR HR airspace becomes Class G" (Moorabbin, Bankstown, Archerfield, Parafield, Jandakot, Camden and others) the zone is shown as class G. Avalon's zone is also dropped after hours, leaving the DAH's class E steps above 700 ft AGL. Reversions with conditions (Albury, Gold Coast, Tamworth and others) stay as published.
    • You can force Tower or CTAF in Radio calls.

    Public holidays and NOTAM changes to tower hours aren't known to the app. Confirm on the ATIS.

    Runways and circuits

    • Diagram: runways to scale with their designators. You're an arrow, or on the edge with your distance when further out, so you can see which side you're joining from. It follows the map's track-up or north-up setting.
    • List: designator, length, surface and right-hand circuits, e.g. "17L/35R 1,341 m sealed 35R RH". Tap an RH tag for ERSA's conditions. Other circuits are left-hand unless ERSA says otherwise.
    • Right-hand circuits from ERSA, including "all circuits to the WEST" notes (Lilydale: RWY 18 right, 36 left by day; the reverse at night).
    • Source: DAH runway thresholds for certified aerodromes; others drawn from ERSA's bearing and length (or the designator), dashed and labelled "schematic".

    ERSA FAC sheet

    Under the name, ERSA FAC YMMB · 7 pages opens that aerodrome's ERSA pages in the app, scrolled to its entry. Zoom doubles the width; Open PDF hands it to the phone's own viewer. It's from the same ERSA cycle as the frequencies. Each sheet is stored when first opened, and those for your plan and Go to aerodromes are fetched in the background with signal so they open offline. Aerodromes without an ERSA entry have no button.

    Weather

    From the NAIPS briefing you load (tick weather in the NAIPS area briefing). It ages with the briefing: check the time on each report.

    • ATIS first: from the briefing (runway, wind, cloud, QNH, operational information; changed fields highlighted; an old ATIS flagged), or ATIS heard hhmmZ when you captured it with Listen to ATIS (see ATIS capture).
    • Area QNH for the area.
    • METAR decoded: wind, QNH, temperature and dew point, density altitude at the aerodrome, and the crosswind and headwind for each runway (magnetic wind, best runway first).
    • TAF as an hour-by-hour table (see TAF decoder). The raw text is under Original.

    NOTAMs

    A NOTAMs box with this aerodrome's NOTAMs from your briefing. It opens by itself when something is closed or unserviceable. Key words such as CLSD, U/S, RWY, FREQ, ACT and PJE are highlighted. Times are UTC with local time in brackets; NOTAMs starting within 24 hours are faded. If the briefing lists the aerodrome under "no current NOTAM", it says "none current". Restricted and danger areas show their status from the briefing with the reason.

    AIP Supplements and AICs

    Under the NOTAMs, AIP SUP / AIC lists the current AIP Supplements and Aeronautical Information Circulars for this aerodrome (its code in the title, e.g. SUP H62/26 Melbourne/Moorabbin Aerodrome (YMMB) Obstacle Infringements), and ones placed within 5 NM of it (a wind farm, a reporting point), with the dates they're in force and expire (estimated).

    • They come from Airservices' Summary of SUP/AIC current built into the app with each AIRAC: titles and dates only. Read the SUP or AIC itself on the Airservices website (www.airservicesaustralia.com/aip/aip.asp).
    • Items placed by a place name in their title (not an aerodrome code) are marked approximate.
    • As the summary itself says, a SUP or AIC can start or end between summaries: check the website and NOTAMs.

    Arrival card

    Arrival (next to the ERSA button in the card's header), or Arrival card on the INBOUND CALL row, opens one screen for the aerodrome:

    • ETA: along your flight plan, else direct at your groundspeed.
    • Frequencies in calling order: ATIS (and whether you have the current letter), the tower (both sides for a split tower) or, when the tower is closed at your ETA, the CTAF, then ground, each with the call sign ("Moorabbin Tower", "Tyabb traffic"); the squawk when the tower is open.
    • Runways for the wind: the wind from the ATIS you heard (Listen to ATIS), the briefing's ATIS, a recent METAR or the TAF at your ETA, in that order; each runway end with its head or tail wind and crosswind, most headwind first and ticked; and its circuit direction: right-hand where ERSA says so, otherwise left-hand (the standard). The ATIS or the traffic in the circuit decides the runway in use.
    • ERSA notes the app picks out (circuit height, helicopter notes, CTR entry, reporting points), the aerodrome's NOTAMs and SUPs/AICs, and hover performance at your ETA when your W&B profile has hover tables.

    Frequency stack

    The airspace stack above your position from the DAH steps: class G uses the area (FIA) frequency, class E the E frequency, C and D the controlling unit. Your current band is highlighted. At Avalon: SFC–A025 D Avalon Tower 120.1, A025–A045 E Avalon 133.55, A045+ C Melbourne Approach 132.0.

    Nearby

    The Nearby list (its own tab on the full-screen map) shows aerodromes around you with distance and bearing. Tap one to show it on the card.
